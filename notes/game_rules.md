GAME OVERVIEW

Cowboy is a highly strategic, elimination-style card game focused on trading, bluffing, and resource management. Players try to avoid holding the lowest card at the end of each round. The game features two distinct modes of play: Normal Mode and Cowboy Mode, which drastically alter card rankings and player options.

COMPONENTS

DECK
    One standard 52-card deck.
    Jokers are removed.

LIVES
    Each player begins with 3 lives (represented by cash, chips, or tees).
    Number of starting lives can be modified by group agreement prior to the game.

TOKENS
    Each player begins the game with exactly two single-use tokens:
    1 Exemption Token (typically a Red chip)
    1 Blocker Token (typically a White chip)
    Tokens are not replenished during the game. Once spent, they are gone permanently.
    If a custom game is played with only 1 Life, Exemption Tokens are removed from the game setup.

CARD RANKINGS

NORMAL ROUND RANKINGS
    Highest to Lowest: Queen, Jack, 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, Ace.
    Note: Kings do not exist in the Normal Round rankings because revealing a King immediately triggers a Cowboy Round. Ace is the absolute worst card in a Normal Round.

COWBOY ROUND RANKINGS
    Highest to Lowest: Ace, King, Queen, Jack, 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2.
    Note: Ace flips from being the lowest card to the highest card. King is the second-highest card. 2 is the absolute worst card.

DEALING PHASE

A Dealer Chip is assigned to a random player for the first round.
The deck is shuffled.
The dealer deals exactly one card face-down to each player.
Dealing starts with the player to the immediate left of the dealer and moves clockwise.
The dealer is dealt the final card.

THE KING TRIGGER
    Players may look at their cards immediately.
    If any player is dealt a King, they must immediately reveal it face-up and shout "Cowboy!"
    If a King is revealed during the deal, the game immediately skips the Normal Round trading phase and enters a Cowboy Round.
    Kings cannot be hidden. Hiding a King is an illegal move and results in public shaming and a round re-deal or penalty.

GAMEPLAY: NORMAL ROUND

TRIGGER
    A Normal Round occurs if no player is dealt a King during the dealing phase.

TURN ORDER
    Play begins with the player to the immediate left of the Dealer Chip.
    Play proceeds clockwise.
    The Dealer is always the last player to act.

PLAYER ACTIONS (NON-DEALER)
    When it is a player's turn, they evaluate their face-down card and must choose one of two actions:

    1. PASS: The player keeps their card. Their turn ends. Play passes to the left.

    2. TRADE: The player proposes a forced trade of their card with the player to their immediate left.

THE BLOCKER TOKEN (DEFENSE)
    If Player A proposes a trade to Player B (the player to their left), Player B may choose to use their Blocker Token.
    If Player B uses their Blocker Token, they surrender the token to the center, and the trade is canceled.
    Player A is now stuck with their card. Player A's turn ends immediately.
    Player B cannot use a Blocker Token preemptively; it can only be used when a trade is actively proposed against them.

THE DEALER'S TURN
    The Dealer is the final player to act.
    Because there is no player left in the round to trade with, the Dealer has two options:

    1. PASS: The dealer keeps their card.

    2. TAKE ONE OFF THE TOP: The dealer discards their card and draws the top card from the remaining deck. This drawn card is immediately revealed face-up to the table.

    DEALER KING TRIGGER
        If the dealer draws a King from the top of the deck, the dealer immediately shouts "Cowboy!"
        The Normal Round instantly ends.
        All previous trades remain locked in place.
        The game immediately transitions into a Cowboy Round.

GAMEPLAY: COWBOY ROUND

TRIGGER
    A Cowboy Round is triggered either instantly during the Deal (if a player is dealt a King) OR at the very end of a Normal Round (if the Dealer draws a King off the top of the deck).

THE EXEMPTION VOTE
    Because Cowboy Round rankings apply (Ace is High, 2 is Low), players must now decide if they want to risk staying in the round or flee.
    No trading occurs in a Cowboy Round.

    All players (regardless of whether they have a token or not) hold their empty or token-holding hands above the table.
    The table counts together: "1... 2... 3... Drop!"
    Players simultaneously open their hands/drop their chips onto the table.

    If a player drops an Exemption Token:
        They fold their card face-down.
        They are "Exempt" and are 100% safe from losing a life this round.
        The Exemption Token is surrendered permanently.

    If a player drops nothing:
        They are "Active" and must participate in the Showdown.

    A player holding a King may legally choose to exempt.

SHOWDOWN AND LOSING A LIFE

After trading in a Normal Round, or after the Exemption Vote in a Cowboy Round, all Active players reveal their cards.

The player holding the lowest ranking Active card loses one life.
If multiple Active players tie for the lowest card, all tying players lose one life.
Exempted players cards are never revealed and cannot lose a life.

THE LONE COWBOY RULE
    In a Cowboy Round, if all players choose to Exempt EXCEPT for the player(s) holding the King, the King becomes the lowest Active card by default.
    The player(s) holding the King will lose a life, despite holding a highly ranked card.

END OF ROUND
    After lives are lost, all cards are collected.
    The Dealer Chip passes one position clockwise to the next surviving player.
    The deck is reshuffled for the next deal.

ELIMINATION AND RESURRECTION

ELIMINATION
    If a player's life total reaches 0, they are eliminated from the game.
    They must wait until the entire game concludes and resets.
    The game continues with the remaining surviving players.

VICTORY
    The game is won when only one player remains alive.

THE RESURRECTION EVENT (TIE-BREAK)
    A Resurrection is triggered ONLY if a tie at the Showdown results in ALL remaining active players losing their final life simultaneously (meaning the game would end with zero survivors).

    Example: Two players remain. Each has 1 life. They both hold a 5. They both lose a life.
    Example: Four players remain. Each has 1 life. They all hold Cowboys and do not exempt. They all lose a life.

    When this occurs, a Draw is declared.
    ALL players who started the game (including previously eliminated players) are Resurrected.
    Every player is brought back into the game with exactly 1 Life.
    Tokens are NEVER returned during a Resurrection. Players return with 0 tokens.
    The Dealer Chip passes to the next sequential player, and a new round begins as a sudden-death match.

Cowboy
======

The game of Cowboy is a card game that uses one standard deck, without any jokers. It's shuffled before cards are distributed to players each round.

Each player is given 3 lives (in person this is 3x one dollar bills that you pay into the pot, or 3 golf tees if you're playing for a singular prize). They're also given two tokens. 1) (often color red) is a cowboy exemption token. 2) (often white) a block token.

(Note: The players may choose to only have two lives per round. If they choose one life per round, then a cowboy exemption token is removed since everyone will use it to avoid dying in the first cowboy round).

Similar to poker, there is a dealer chip that moves over one player after each round. The person to the left of the dealer chip is dealt the first card, and then dealt to the next person to the left, and this pattern follows until the player with the dealer chip. AKA (dealer is dealt last).

At the start of a game, each player has their lives and tokens. A game then has many rounds until there is only one player with lives left.

At the start of each round, each player receives one card face down so only they can see it.

...If _any_ player is holding a King, they shout "cowboy!" and they reveal their king. More than one person might have a king as there are 4 in the deck. Several may shout cowboy! at the same time. Kings are never hidden. So if 3 players are dealt a king, you'll see 3 kings face up with everyone else still face down.

During a cowboy round, 2 is the lowest card and Ace is highest. Any players who want to may choose to exempt from the cowboy round and in a group we hold are hands up (whether you have a red chip or not) and on 1...2...3... we drop them on the table simultaneous. After this simultaneous drop you cannot exempt. It's basically a quick think and vote period. We do this so you cannot react to what other players are doing as an advantage. Those who exempted are safe. Once you spend your exemption token, it's gone for the entire game. The folks _with_ a cowboy could technically exempt if they wanted to from the round, even though a King (cowboy) is the second highest card in this type of round. Players who already exempted cannot participate in future exemption votes during a cowboy round. There is no undo button when it comes to exempting. If one player exempts and only them, they cannot undo it.

Players who exempt fold their cards. The values are never seen. They are safe.

Any player who choose to NOT exempt must turn their cards over. The player with the lowest card loses a life. This might mean multiple people lose a life if say multiple people have the same lowest card.

NOTE: If everyone exempts EXCEPT the player with a king (cowboy), then THEY will lose a life. Cowboy doesn't guarantee you're safe. It just means you're in a cowboy round, though it's usually a safe position. Players tend to exmpet heavily at the first opportunity and going forward cowboy becomes remarkably safe, but it's all strategy.

...If NO players have a cowboy (king) then we conduct a normal round.

In a normal round, the player who was dealt the first card chooses to either pass (meaning they're good) or trade their card with the person to their left.

If the player to the left has a blocker token, and the active player decides to trade with them, the player on the left can choose to use their blocker token to keep their card. If they do NOT have a blocker token remaining, they must trade with the active player.

Note: A player will only be able to block if a trade is poposed. Otherwise, a preemptive block could reveal that you have a good card! But also, reveal to the active player that they _would_ block and from a strategy perspective, maybe they ask to trade just to force you to block (and become defensively weakened) even if they weren't planning on it.

Once a blocker token is spent, it's gone for the rest of the game. You can only use a blocker token one time.

After, the it's the next player's turn (player to the left) who begins the same process all over again.

This continues until the last player (the one with the dealer chip). They are then asked if they want to pass (keep their card) or "take one off the top" which allows them to trade their card with the top of the deck. If they take one off the top, it's revealed publicly immediately to the whole table.

If the card off the top is not a king (cowboy), then each person reveals their cards to find the lowest scoring player. When we're NOT in a cowboy round, the lowest card is an Ace, then 2, 3, 4, etc... with Queen being the highest. There will never be a king in a non-cowboy round because any king triggers a cowboy round.

If this card is a King (a cowboy) then we launch into a cowboy round as described above. The king will belong to the player that asked for a card off the top. As it would have been if it wasn't a king.

There may be more than one loser if multiple players have the same lowest card.

Each losing player loses a life at the end of a round. If they have no more lives, they're out, and no longer play until the game resets.

Every round will have a loser UNLESS it's a cowboy round and EVERYONE exempts. This means there is nobody left to lose.

At the end of the round, cards are collected and the deck is shuffled, and the dealer chip advances to the next alive player before being dealt to surviving players.

Rounds are played until there is one surviving player who is the winner.

However! There is one unique thing that can happen when there are only two players left. If, on the last round, the two players tie.. be it both cowboy, or both end with a jack, then it's a draw and ALL players come back in with one life. Any tying cards will trigger the ressurection of all players. Players who are resurrected do not get any exemption or blocker tokens. You just get one life back and are dealt into the next round. Just like any normal round, the dealer chip advances to the next surviving player (which would be all of them at this moment), cards are collected and reshuffled, and then dealt to begin the next round.

A tie in cowboy only triggers a resurrection if the surviving players all receive a king and either ALL or NONE can exempt (a tie in outcome).

While we say "two players" left, technically, it could be 4 players remaining who could end up with a tie. This tie among all would trigger a reseruction.

Before starting a game, players can choose the number of lives, blocker tokens, and exemption tokens allowed per player for the game.
